# Changelog

## 0.4.0, 2026-09-15

Three more of the intake answers reach the screen. Cards and play-once: `[show] playback` is `loop` (the default, and unchanged: the file plays on repeat) or `once`, and `[show] title_card` and `end_card` hold the words before and after the show, timed by `card_seconds` (6) and `card_fade_s` (1, at most half of the seconds, or an error naming both keys). A card is a few words on a screen, so it holds at most six lines and 200 characters, each an error naming the key; a card of only whitespace is no card; and an end card under `playback = "loop"` is a warning, not an error, because a looping file never reaches it. The settings reach `handoff/show.json` as `show.playback` and `show.cards` (`title`, `end`, `seconds`, `fade_s`), and `bin/build` carries them into the manifest and reports them. The live player holds the title card, fading it in and out, and then scrolls exactly as before, the clock waiting with the card; with `playback = "once"` the scroll stops after exactly one loop, the end card holds, the page freezes on it and the music pauses once the card is fully in (with no end card the page freezes and says "the end"). `?card=title` and `?card=end` show one card alone, full screen, and set `window.__cardReady`, which is how the render photographs them. `bin/render` puts the cards into the video as their own segments and never draws them over the mosaic: the loop is rendered and verified on its own as `<name>-loop.mp4`, with the wrap check exactly what it was, then each card is screenshotted from the player's card page into `build/cards/<stem>-<which>.png`, checked against the manifest's text, and encoded to `build/cards/<stem>-<which>.mp4` from that still with the loop's own encoder settings, the tv-usb profile and level included, so title, loop and end can be joined by copying the streams into the name the launchers play; the loop file is removed once the joined file's frame count equals the loop's plus the cards'. The soundtrack is then cut to the assembled length, so the music covers the cards. Only a full-loop render assembles cards: a `--seconds` or `--from` render leaves them out, says so when there was a title card to leave out (`cards: skipped (they go on a full-loop render only)`) and is otherwise unchanged, and `--concat` with `playback = "once"` is refused naming both, since copies of a show that ends are not a show. `build/lib/cards.js` is the new module and its comments are the short form of all this. `bin/build` also writes `build/playback.txt`, one line holding `loop` or `once`: the three VLC launchers read it beside the video (the two `.command` ones then in the script's own folder and in `../build`, the `.bat` in its own folder, which is where it plays from) and pass `--repeat` only when it says `loop`; with no file they keep looping, as they always have. Copy it to the stick with the video. The browser launchers need nothing: `player.html` carries the playback mode and both cards in its own manifest.

Captions. `[taste] captions` is `none` (the default), `date` or `text`, and is validated now: an unknown value is an error naming the three, where 0.3 wrote whatever string it found. `date` draws each tile's date at its own precision (`2019`, `June 2021`, `June 15, 2021`); `text` draws the `caption` column of `handoff/media.csv`. `index/items.csv` gains a last column `description`, filled from the export's sidecar description (Takeout JSON and XMP alike), squeezed to one line and trimmed to 200 characters, and the index stage's sidecar line now counts the files that carry one; `media.csv` gains a last column `caption`, carried from `description` by the handoff stage for every selected file whatever the mode is, so the owner can edit it before the build. The player draws the caption in a bar at the bottom of the tile, one line, ellipsised at the tile's width, at 22 px on 1440 rows and scaled with the height; with `?labels=1` it moves to the top of the tile so the review label keeps the bottom. `bin/build` reports `captions: <mode>, N of M tiles` and warns when the mode is `text` and no tile carries one, which means the export had no descriptions. The caption is an element on the page for the live player and the flagging page, and the render's canvas paints the same bar, measuring the text to ellipsise it, so it is in the rendered file as well.

The cap counts in periods. `[selection] period` is `year` (the default, which reproduces 0.3 key for key and number for number), `quarter` or `month`: the bucket the cap counts in, keyed `2020`, `2020-Q2` or `2020-05`. The cap is derived over the period-shares in scope, the scope's own first and last periods pro-rated by their months, or by their days when the unit is a month, so one loop-length target and one scope give 24 per year, 6 per quarter or 2 per month: the loop comes out the same length and the show is cut finer, which is what a trip wants and a whole life does not. `[selection.cap_overrides]` takes a year and sets every period inside it, so a config written for years keeps working when the unit changes, and a key that is not a year in the scope is an error naming the scope; the keying by full period key that `period_caps()` accepts does not reach a run in this version, because `select` and `show` read the same table back in its year-keyed form and stop on one. `select` prints the unit on the cap line (`cap: derived 6 per quarter from a 2-minute target at 2.2 s per item (medium tiles, 50 px/s): 55 moments over 9.3 quarter-shares`), names the unit and the bucket count when it is not the year, and its table's first column is the period key. A dated file whose date is too coarse for the unit, a year-precision file under `period = "month"`, belongs to no bucket and is cut `over-cap`, and the out-of-scope line says so. The contact sheets stay one per year whatever the unit is, since a year is what an owner reviews in one sitting; a year's printed cap is then the sum of the caps of the periods inside it. `[selection] undated_cap` (0) is how many undated files `select --include-undated` may seat: they get a bucket of their own called `undated` and never borrow a dated period's cap, because a file with no date cannot be spread across the show. With 0, as before, an undated file may compete but is never seated, and it is cut `over-cap`; without the flag it is cut `undated` as it always was.

One layout fix is worth a line of its own. With `[taste] mixed_tiles = false` a video is not an anchor, and two of the rules that keep videos apart looked only at anchors: a video could arrive inside the stray items folded into a feature row, or through the pull that fills an underfilled base row, and the build could then put two videos in one row and fail its own acceptance check afterwards. Both rules now count the videos a row already holds (`build/lib/build.js`), and the build warns when `media.csv` still carries featured rows from a selection made with `mixed_tiles = true`, since those take feature rows anyway until `select` and `handoff` are run again. The guards 0.3.0 added are unchanged and still hold: `bin/build` refuses to build on a prep output marked `ok: false` or on clips prepared under the other `slow_motion` mode while a slow-motion capture is in the set, `add-item` refuses to form a Live Photo pair under `live_photos = "still"`, `select` refuses `[family] gate = "people"` when `people.csv` has no person counts, and a prepared music track's name keeps only the characters a relative URL survives.

Two things on the list were deliberately not built, and the reasons are the point. A camera table, mapping make and model to an owner, was left out: gate 1 already requires a second witness on any stem join and already takes the camera make and model as that witness, and a table cannot separate two phones of the same model, which is the case that actually bites (the first run met it). The useful version needs a body serial number, which most exports strip, so a table would look like a safeguard without being one. RAW stills were left out too: every decoder for a proprietary RAW is a compiled dependency this project has kept out on purpose, the install being Python, Node and a static ffmpeg. DNG could go through ffmpeg today; CR2, NEF, ARW and the rest cannot, and half a feature that silently skips most of an owner's RAW files is worse than none. It waits for someone with a real library of RAW files to test against.

Exercised on this version: on Linux, on the two synthetic projects from 0.3, every stage ran through `prep`, `build` and a render on both, the 0.3-shaped config reproducing its picks, rows, frame counts and file names with no card, `playback` loop, `captions` none and `period` year. A full-loop render with a title and an end card wrote both PNGs and both segments, joined title, loop and end, and the finished file carried 1,171 frames, the 871 of the loop between two 150-frame cards, with the music muxed over the assembled length and the card frames checked in the file itself. The play-once page was driven through a real browser from the title card, through one loop, to the end card and its freeze, and `?card=title` and `?card=end` each photographed alone. The card validation stopped the `show` stage on a seventh line, on a card over 200 characters, on `card_fade_s` above half of `card_seconds` and on an unknown `playback`, each naming its key, and an unknown `captions` stopped it naming the three modes it takes. A sidecar description travelled from the export into `items.csv`, into `media.csv`, into the manifest and onto the page, with the build's count line and its empty-set warning, and a render-mode frame was captured from the canvas with every tile's date on it, so the captions are in the video and not only on the page. The period unit ran at year, quarter and month over one scope (24, 6 and 2 per bucket) with an override applying to all four quarters of its year when it was written as the year and to one quarter when it was written as `"2020-Q2"`, and the undated cap ran at absent, 0 and 2, seating nothing under the first two and seating from the undated bucket under the third, the cut reason `over-cap` with the flag and `undated` without it. Forty fuzzed layouts, with mixed tiles on and off across several video fractions and seeds, produced no acceptance failure and no row with two videos. Not exercised: identify's detection run (no models on this machine), a Takeout zip source, the live player's music in a browser that decodes AAC, the Windows and macOS launchers on real machines, a real TV stick, and a card on any display other than 1280x720.

## 0.2.0, 2026-09-15

The stages are real. `curate/run.py <stage>` runs `ingest`, `index`, `validate`, `identify`, `select`, `sheets` and `handoff` under `curate/stages/`, each wired to `common.py`, resumable, dry-runnable, printing its counts and refusing to write when its accounting does not balance. Build side: every command resolves the project folder (`--project`, `SLIDESHOW_PROJECT`, or the nearest `config.toml`) and never writes into the repository; `add-item` computes `media_id` and writes contract-format `changes.log` lines; `apply-replacements` writes `media.csv` and `changes.log` back into the round folder on every run; Windows `.cmd` wrappers with a `SetThreadExecutionState` keep-awake; Playwright's bundled Chromium tried before installed Chrome.

Tested on Windows with synthetic media only: all seven stages end to end, then `prep` and `build` on the handoff. Untested: ffmpeg paths, detection on real photos, macOS. The legacy scripts stay in `curate/legacy/` until the smoke test passes.
